# 09 · Decision Log

Most of the choices in this research, large and small, are scattered across the chapters where they happened: the choice of scale in 05 and 6.3, the freezes in 6.3 and 6.8, the two rejections in 3.6 and 8.2. This chapter gathers them into a single timeline, and each entry tries to state the same few things: the problem at the time, the evidence relied on, the decision made, and the consequences that followed — a few entries (entry 5, for instance) are background events rather than rulings, and are listed alongside the rest to complete the causal chain. Every ruling that an earlier chapter flagged with "see chapter 09" traces back to an entry here.

One point of authority first: every decision was made by a human; the AI handled proposals, execution, and checking the evidence (Appendix F covers this uniformly). This chapter is the ledger of those calls.

## 9.1 The Log Entries

**1｜July 8｜Research first, build second.** The first decision after the project restarted: investigate before building. Four rounds of investigation — a domain scan, gap-filling on paradigms, a depth assessment, and implementation references — were completed within days (2.2, chapter 3). The evidence (3.2, 3.3): the score-based route was backed by 40 items of literature and practice records, the lap-time route by only 5; and that deep-network paper was rejected after the original was read — its actual method is infeasible under single-digit race samples. **Decision**: score-based first, lap times kept as a conditional layer (2.2); no deep networks in the first version. **Consequence**: the entire methodology (chapter 04) rests on this judgment; the trigger conditions for the lap-time layer have yet to mature (8.4).

**2｜July 17｜The candidate registry.** Problem: in the predecessor project, models came from scattered sources and scores could not be traced back to configurations. The alternative was to keep running under the old naming — rejected, because then no score could be attributed to a source. **Decision**: establish a candidate registry; the first 29 candidates were registered in numbered batches over a few days and assigned into 8 related groups (the record landed on July 17); the same day, the old calibration data was moved out of the run path, so that legacy residue could not contaminate new evaluations. **Consequence**: from then on, every score could be pinned to a candidate with a number, a configuration, and a provenance; the finding that "31 candidates produced only 17 to 19 distinct rankings" (6.4) depends on the family information the registry recorded.

**3｜July 19–20｜One scoring standard.** Problem: the old metrics MAE and NDCG each had blind spots, and running several scales in parallel left "which is better" without a single answer. Evidence: a blind-spot audit of the two old metrics and a parameter sensitivity check (6.3). **Decision**: the C0 mean became the sole criterion and MAE/NDCG were retired; the evaluation window was fixed at R03–R09 — R01 lacks part of the manual data, and R02, as the starting point of usable data, carries no accumulated historical features at all, so only from R03 onward do predictions have a comparable basis (6.3); one scoring preference anchor accepted a compromise: the blending coefficient was relaxed from 0.432 to 0.20, one gap narrowed from 0.0358 to 0.0078, and the costs at two other places are on record (5.4). **Consequence**: "change the model, never the scale" has held since; the full rerun of September 18 (entry 10) is exactly this frozen scale recomputed on corrected data.

**4｜July 20–21｜Qualifying iteration frozen.** Once the unified standard was in place, the qualifying tuning iteration (the batch-numbered ST series) converged in its sixth round: the last two products — slot ensemble candidates #30 and #31 — were merged into the registry, 29 becoming 31; the same day it was ruled that the iteration would stop there, with no further tuning. **Why then**: every genuine round-over-round improvement in a candidate had already passed the test of "no worse than the baseline on the other seven rounds" (the LOEO standard of 6.3); continuing to tune would only begin fitting noise inside the window. **Consequence**: note that what was frozen here is the "tuning iteration", not the evaluation — candidates went on being evaluated round by round as usual (when 7.4 says "the qualifying line has no corresponding freeze action", it means it never entered a prospective protocol of the race-line kind; the two lines differ in shape); and the record in 7.3 that "the within-window lead did not fully carry out of the window" was not retroactively altered precisely because of this freeze.

**5｜July 25–28｜The first step out of the training window.** Right after the freeze came a decision: extend the evaluation to real rounds outside the window. The two single-round replays of R10 and R11 were the first move of that expansion (6.4). **Consequence**: the first signal of #31's late-stage decline was picked up; and the circuit history files generated on the spot by those two replays later became a data source for the September 18 full rerun.

**6｜August 2｜The race model changes design.** Problem: the race model ran into "the monotone-transform problem" at the outset — every feature correlated strongly with grid position, and linear corrections could not move the ranking (6.7). After the diagnosis, the design changed: standardization plus a single interaction term (strong teams recovering from the back of the grid); the R10–R11 test score moved from 0.5438 to 0.5917 (6.7). **Consequence**: the race line now had an approach independent of the qualifying line; and the mechanistic hypothesis that "strong teams at the back will recover" was later borne out in the most extreme way at R13 Monza (7.2).

**7｜The last week of August｜The freeze.** The race weights (recent form 3.0, recovery rate 2.0, rear-of-grid interaction 3.0) and the formula were frozen in full (6.8), and R12 (Zandvoort) became the first unseen round after the freeze; the notion of "the third window" — every race run after the freeze counts — was established with this freeze. **A factual note**: this freeze left no independent ruling document; the date follows the narrative in 6.8 — it is a counterexample to this project's habit of leaving a record, and 9.2 returns to that lesson.

**8｜September 5｜Two rejections.** First, the constructor-strength correction mechanism: two rounds of isolated experiments (the first with six correction variants, the second a gated refresh — six versions compared side by side, three of them switched to fabricated-event data as controls) showed a best gain of +0.007, below the pre-set +0.010 threshold, and one fabricated-event control gained even more than the real-event group — the mechanism does not enter the main line. The mechanism definition and the blind-test pre-registration were locked together: each team independently maintains a ledger of upgrade events; the refresh begins once an event has completed its gated window (k=3 rounds); the refresh uses progressive blending — races completed after the upgrade are counted as post, the new value carries weight post/(post+3) and the old value 3/(post+3), transitioning gradually as races accumulate rather than switching in one step; two trigger rounds were pre-registered (McLaren tied to R14, Aston Martin to R15), and at a trigger round the matter is revisited only if the score gap between "correction on" and "correction off" reaches +0.010 — if it does not, the topic is closed until the next rules cycle. Second, the lineup-change mechanism: the proposal was to rebuild the scoring universe dynamically for drivers who switch teams or substitute; the ruling was no mechanism (final) — patching the lineup assumption amounts to rewriting the definition of "who is on track" at every round, and the end-to-end traceability of candidate scores would cease to exist (7.5, 8.3). **Consequence**: the blind test is the follow-up to the first rejection (at the time of writing the trigger verdict has not yet been registered; 8.2 records this late registration as it stands); the way out of the second is not a mechanism but a redesign (the "dynamic lineup" item in 8.4).

**9｜September 4–14｜Prospective runs and one judgment revision.** Data for R13 (Monza) and R14 (Madrid) was backfilled into the archive, and the incremental evaluation of both lines ran as usual (7.2, 7.3). In the interim, an FIA appeal ruling changed the race classification of R06 (Monaco): Gasly moved from P3 to P7, Hadjar rose to P3, with the standings at that point carried along. **Decision**: the actuals were re-collected into the archive, while the archived scores already on file were not refreshed retroactively — a full recomputation was considered at the time and declined, on the grounds of keeping "each artifact matched to the conditions under which it was produced", so that published scorecards would not quietly change shape; the judgment revision affects only runs after it. The exclusion of R01 and R02 is unrelated to this revision and stands unchanged. **Consequence**: a known time gap opened between the early-September qualifying scorecard and the revised actuals; the next entry closes that gap.

**10｜September 18｜The full rerun.** A routine check during the writing period (7.3, note 1) escalated into a data audit: the two July replays and the early-September merged scorecard did not agree on the numbers, because the two sets of runs could read different ranges of data — the circuit history feature was missing files from R10 onward, so in the early-September version every candidate depending on that feature was effectively answering with one reference fewer; and that version had run before the R06 judgment revision and re-collection, so the revision never entered its feature chain. **Decision**: the missing circuit history archives were completed (two from the July replay artifacts, two generated offline from already-archived raw data), everything was recomputed under uniform conditions, and the old and new scorecards were kept side by side in the archive. **Consequence**: the official qualifying scorecard changed version, with #31 rising to first in the merged standings at 0.4835 (its first-seven-rounds 0.5103 unchanged); the time gap of entry 9 closed there. One process lesson came with it: check the completeness of the input directories before running an evaluation — a missing file raises no error at the time; it only leaves a feature silently absent.

**11｜September 7｜A proposal that was not approved.** The first thing racing fans ask about any qualifying model is whether it reads the practice sessions. Fact, verified entry by entry on this day: all 31 candidates use zero practice data — the feature set is recent form, same-circuit history, constructor strength, circuit fit, track profile, pre-race evidence and reliability. The community genuinely splits on the question: one side holds that practice running — different programmes, fuel loads, unfamiliar parts — says nothing about qualifying; the other, that this only means you have not looked properly. On this day an isolated experiment on FP relative pace was proposed: it was not approved and has not run. **Decision**: record the proposal and its non-approval; make no claim in either direction. **Consequence**: inside this research, "practice would help" and "practice is noise" are both beliefs, not findings — the question stays open (8.1), and the data to test it is already on file (8.4).

## 9.2 The Procedure Behind the Rulings

Read together, the ten entries show three practices recurring. **Isolated experiments**: a new idea does not go straight into the model; it is first compared side by side with the current version in a side branch (the two experiment rounds of entry 8), and stays in the side branch if it misses the bar. **Pre-registration**: every promise to "verify later" locks the criteria before looking at the data (the blind test of entry 8: trigger rounds, parameters, and thresholds all fixed ahead of the outcome), guarding against cherry-picking after the fact. **Quantified thresholds**: +0.010 is not necessarily the optimal threshold, but it turns "feels useful" into a proposition that evidence can vote down — the first rejection of entry 8 was made by exactly this means.

Their boundary of application: they constrain the replacement of models and methods, not data corrections — the rerun of entry 10 needed no pre-registration, because "fix the data when it is wrong" is not model selection. Issues discovered after the freeze (the R14 narrowing, the knock-on effects of lineup changes) are handled by putting them on record: matters of model and method are left for reconsideration (6.8); matters of principled ruling (no mechanism for the lineup) are final — the condition for reopening them is not new data but a change in the design's foundations (the "dynamic lineup" redesign in 8.4 is currently the only positive exit on record).

"Judgment by humans" also has a concrete procedure, rather than being a stance: the AI produces the proposals and checks the evidence, forming a decision record with options and grounds; a human rules; the ruling lands in versioned records and state files, traceable afterwards (Appendix F). The qualifying iteration freeze, the two rejections, and the full rerun all went down this path. The one not yet completed is the trigger verdict of the R14 blind test — it will be entered retroactively together with its grounds (the Appendix E referred to in 8.2), and the backfilled entry itself must state its registration time honestly.

Finally, the "shortest reason" quoted in 7.5. Why no mechanism for lineup changes: every score in this research rests on the premise of "one scoring universe run from start to finish"; patch for team switches and substitutes, and the premise is gone. The cost is a three-layer chain (six candidates unscorable, new signings necessarily missed, feature mismatch for switched drivers — 7.5); this research keeps the cost, and keeps the traceability. The remaining open items are listed in 8.4 and are not repeated here; each of them will in time be added to this ledger as an entry in the same format.
